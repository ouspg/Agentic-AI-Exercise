Day 8

My last day had a bittersweet energy; bright and sunny, but with the awareness that time was running out. The weather stayed warm and welcoming, and the sky looked clean, almost like it was trying to give me one final perfect postcard view.

I started with something I hadn’t fully done yet: shopping and wandering through a market area. It felt lively in a comfortable way. People were chatting and vendors were setting out fruit and handmade goods. The air was filled with small bursts of different smells. I tasted a few treats, picked up souvenirs that felt personal rather than generic, and talked to a couple of friendly people who pointed out places I should’ve seen earlier.

In the afternoon, I returned to the beach one more time. I picked a spot that let me see both the shoreline and the open ocean, and I just sat there for a while. It wasn’t about doing an activity; it was about letting the trip settle into my brain. I watched kids play, couples walk hand-in-hand, and surfers bob in the water as if waiting for a perfect wave.

Then I went for a final swim. The water still felt inviting, and I felt surprisingly calm, like my body recognized it was coming to the end and wanted to savor the last bit. The sun was lower now, and the light made everything look warmer than it actually was.

As evening came, I found a place to watch the last sunset of the trip. The sky stretched into deep colors again—pink and orange melting into the horizon. I didn’t take as many photos as usual; I just watched.

That night I ate dinner a little slower than before and let myself feel grateful. I packed my things carefully, knowing I’d probably unpack this trip into memories instead of just a suitcase.
