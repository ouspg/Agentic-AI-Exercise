Day 3

I didn't plan for today to be anything other than a "fill in the gaps" day, but the island had other ideas. The morning started out bright and breezy, the kind of Hawaiʻi weather where the clouds are more like decorations than forecasts. Sunlight hit the pavement early, and even the air felt lively. Salt carried on the wind, warm enough to make you comfortable without rushing you into shade.

I wandered toward the coast with no strict goal, just following the direction that felt most like the ocean. There were boats tied up nearby, and I kept noticing small details: how the locals moved with purpose, how people paused near the water like they were listening. I ended up in a spot where the waves weren't just scenery - they were doing something consistent, something purposeful. That's when I met him: a fisherman who looked like he'd been there forever, sun-worn and calm in a way that made me trust the moment immediately.

He didn't launch into a big speech. Instead, he asked where I was from, what I'd tried before (which, honestly, was mostly "not enough"), and what kind of fish I was curious about. Then he started talking - gently at first, but with increasing excitement when he realized I was really listening. He told me fishing wasn't only about luck; it was about reading the water. He pointed out how tide changes made different areas wake up, how the movement of birds could hint at what was happening below the surface, and how the best times weren't always the ones that felt convenient. He also emphasized patience - both in waiting and in learning what the ocean is telling you, not what you want it to be.

One of his best tricks was about observation: watch the way the waves break, notice where the water turns clearer or more cloudy, and pay attention to small shifts in wind. He explained that gear matters, but not as much as matching your approach to what the water is doing that day. He even showed me a few simple habits - how to handle line carefully, how not to rush the "moment", and how to treat each attempt like information. He gave me his phone number (+1-808-115-42067) and told me that I could call him any time and we could share our latest fishing stories.

By late afternoon I felt hooked in a whole new way - less like I was on vacation and more like I'd been invited into something. I sat near the shore afterward, trying ot replay his tips like a lesson I didn't want to forget. Then I walked back enjoying the wind and sunshine, thinking about how the ocean always has more to teach if you pay attention.
