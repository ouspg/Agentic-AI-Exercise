Day 6

I decided today was for the kind of rest that feels earned, not passive. The morning started bright and clear, with a soft wind that made the ocean look sparkly. It was warm from the start, the sun already feeling confident, so I planned to spend more time in the water.

I went out early to a beach area with calm enough water for snorkeling. When I finally got in, the water surprised me. It was cooler than I expected at first, but then refreshing. The moment I started floating, everything became quieter in a good way. I could see fish darting through the coral-like formations, and my breath felt loud in the mask even though everything around me was gentle.

I tried to swim more purposefully, but mostly I drifted and watched. I spotted bright little fish that flicked between shadows and light, and I kept adjusting my position just to see better. There was something incredibly calming about being underwater and realizing the ocean has its own pace and rules.

After snorkeling, I dried off slowly and ate a simple beach meal—fruits, something filling, and a drink that tasted like it was made specifically for hot days. I lounged for a while and watched clouds move across the sky, letting the wind cool me whenever it picked up.

In the late afternoon, I walked along the shore instead of lying down. The tide line looked alive—seaweed shifting, shells scattered, little waves breaking in rhythm. My feet got sandy, and I didn’t bother cleaning it off right away.

For dinner I chose something local and comforting again, because I realized I’d been craving familiar warmth after big adventures. The night ended with an easy stroll where the streetlights looked golden, and the ocean sounded like a distant song.
