Day 4

I woke up to a sky that looked extra crisp, like someone turned up the contrast on the day. The weather was still warm, definitely beach weather, but the wind seemed stronger. That always makes me think the North Shore is going to be more dramatic, and today didn’t disappoint.

I drove north with snacks and music and kept stopping at pull-offs when the view looked like a postcard. The coastline changed in personality the farther I went: fewer city lights, more open ocean, and waves that looked powerful even from a distance. I watched surfers out at the breaks. Some surfers were already carving through the water while others waited like they were listening for the right moment.

By midday I walked along the beach and took my time. The sand felt a little different than Waikīkī—coarser, more “real,” less curated. The sun was bright, and I could tell I’d need sunscreen again even if I hadn’t planned to stay out long. I just kept thinking how the ocean looked like it was moving even when it wasn’t. White foam appearing and disappearing with each set of waves.

I ate at a laid-back spot I’d heard about and tried local-style food that tasted like someone made it with pride and no shortcuts. After lunch I explored a few small areas, took photos, and wandered through roadside stands that had everything from fruit to handmade items.

Late afternoon, clouds rolled in just enough to soften the sun but not enough to ruin the day. The wind cooled my skin in the best way. I spent the last stretch of daylight watching the waves and then drove back toward home with the feeling that I’d gotten a whole different Hawaiʻi today.

That night, I slept harder than usual, like my body understood it had been active and happy at the same time.
