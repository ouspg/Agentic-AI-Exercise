Day 5

Today the weather surprised me. I started with warmth and clear skies, but by the time I got closer to the interior, the air felt thicker and cooler - like the island’s mood had shifted. The clouds were lower, the greens were deeper, and the whole landscape looked sharper, more alive, as if everything had been watered from the inside.

I spent the day exploring volcanic and rainforest areas. The roads wound through dense vegetation, and I kept looking out the window because the view kept changing every few minutes. Small waterfalls, tall trees, and dark rock formations that made the place feel ancient. When I stepped out at overlooks, the wind carried that earthy scent that always reminds me of rain that might happen soon.

At one point I walked a trail where everything sounded different: birds calling, leaves rustling, and the occasional distant roar of something bigger than my day-to-day worries. The ground underfoot felt solid, but I still moved carefully, like the landscape deserved respect. The air was damp on my skin, and my hair felt slightly heavier, as if it had absorbed the humidity.

The views tied it together; massive horizons broken up by cliffs, crater-like formations, and that dramatic sense of scale. Even when I tried to focus on taking pictures, I kept forgetting the camera and just looking with my whole attention.

I ate lunch with views and ended up lingering longer than planned, because the light was changing fast and everything looked moody and cinematic. In the late afternoon, it sprinkled a little. It rained just enough to make the air smell even better.

By evening, I returned to more familiar comfort. The sky was calmer, and my muscles felt that gentle “I did something today” soreness. I showered, ate something satisfying, and fell asleep thinking about how powerful the island felt, even when I was just standing still.
